I am making a game. It is a story game in which a vending machine serves people, and gets to know those people through the conversations they have while they are at the machine. The people can arrive alone, or in small groups (say 2-3 coworkers at lunch). The stories will unfold at subsequent visits. There should also be some interconnectedness, as a person might not always use the vending machine with the same group of people, and in some cases we should have the ability to see a story unfold from multiple perspectives (for example both parites in a relationship use the vending machine at different times). 

The setting for this is porpusefully kept a little vague, but it takes place towards the outer part of a large city, sometimes in the late 90s/early 2000s (so the internet and cell phones are commonplace, but we are definitely in the past) Give me ideas for people and their stories.





## 1. The Aspiring Musician and the Skeptical Sibling
(Note: good mid-length story, with characters we can root for. Maya needs to be tied into something else)
**Characters:**
- Leo (21): An optimistic, slightly scruffy musician. Always talking about his band's next gig, a new song he wrote, or the "big break" that's just around the corner. Often buys cheap chips.
- Maya (24): Leo's older sister. Works in a corporate office nearby. She's pragmatic and worried about Leo's future. She loves him but wishes he'd get a "real job". Buys diet soda and a granola bar.
**Story Arc:**
- Maya and Leo visit together. She's giving him money for lunch and nagging him about finding a stable job. He's trying to convince her to come to his next show.
- Leo visits alone, humming a new tune, excited because a "real producer" is coming to their next gig.
- Maya visits with a coworker, venting her worries about Leo. She's afraid he'll be disappointed again.
- Leo visits after the gig, looking dejected. The producer didn't show up. He buys the same diet soda his sister always gets.
- Maya visits alone, on her flip phone, leaving a supportive voicemail for Leo, telling him not to give up and that she's proud of him.
**Player Interactivity**
- Cheer Up Leo, Change Maya's Mind, Get Leo the Break

## 2. The Office Romance
(Note: Perfect long term story that can include anything. Both characters needs to be tied into something else)
**Characters:**
- Clara (30s): A sharp, witty graphic designer.
- Ben (30s): A quiet, thoughtful programmer from another department.
**Story Arc:**
- Clara and Ben "run into each other" at the machine. Their conversation is awkward but friendly. They talk about a project they are both peripherally on.
- Clara comes with a friend, gushing about the "cute programmer" she met. Her friend teases her.
- Ben comes with his deskmate, nervously asking for advice on how to ask a coworker out without making it weird.
- Clara and Ben come together, clearly on a break. The conversation is much more relaxed and personal. He buys her favorite candy bar after she mentions it off-hand.
- We see them separately over the next few visits, talking on their phones to friends about their first date, a minor disagreement, and plans for the weekend. This allows the player to see the relationship develop from both perspectives.
**Player Interactivity**
- ??? (influence them over the course of the relationship)

## 4. The Secret Novelist
(Note: Good isolated long term story)
**Characters:** 
- Evelyn (40s): An unassuming data entry clerk. She's quiet and keeps to herself. She is secretly writing a fantasy novel on her lunch breaks.
**Story Arc:** Her visits are her main break from writing. At first, she mutters character names or plot points to herself. Later, she might have a brief, awkward conversation with someone that gives her an idea. We see her dejection after getting a rejection letter via email on her phone, and later, her quiet, tearful joy when she gets an offer from a small publisher.
**Player Interactivity**
- Literally help her write the story, also maybe reveal self?
  
## 5. The Rival Salesmen
**Characters:**
- Gary (30s): Slick, confident, always boasting about his numbers.
- Mark (30s): Equally competitive but more stressed and less flashy.
**Story Arc:** 
- Their initial interactions are tense, full of passive-aggressive jabs about clients and commissions. 
- Then, Mark hits a slump. We see him alone, looking defeated. Gary sees this over a few visits. 
- In a surprising turn, Gary "accidentally" buys two drinks and offers one to Mark, giving him a small, gruff piece of advice that helps him get back on his feet.
**Player Interactivity**
- Just make them feel better, be better people.

## 7. The Conspiracy Theorist
(Notes: maybe switch gender with the intern)
**Characters:** 
- Dale (40s): Works in the records department. He's convinced the company is a front for something more sinister.
**Story Arc:** Dale tries to find patterns in everything. 
- He'll point out "suspicious" new snack additions to anyone who will listen. 
- He tries to recruit Samir (the IT guy) to help him "access the real servers." 
- Most people dismiss him, but one day he finds a strange, old document that seems to prove one of his minor theories, leading to a small, personal victory that only he and the vending machine know about.
**Player Interactivity**

## 8. The Long-Distance Couple's Local Half

## 9. The Recent Divorcee
**Characters:** 
- Robert (50s): A manager who is newly divorced. He's trying to navigate being single for the first time in 25 years.
**Story Arc:** 
- Initially, he comes to the machine with a coworker, talking about the logistics of the divorce. 
- In subsequent solo visits, we see him trying to figure out modern dating via his phone, awkwardly asking younger colleagues for advice, and talking to his adult children. His story is one of rediscovery and fumbling towards a new life.
  
## 10. The Building's "Gossip Queen"
(Notes: while I don't like this exact character, there could be merit in a character that knows almost everyone, and sort of ties the whole thing together. Maybe a parking lot security guard?)
**Character:** 
- Brenda (50s): Works in HR. She knows everyone and everything.
