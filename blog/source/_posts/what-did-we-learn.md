So, a few days passed since the jam. I meant to write this earlier, but the thing is with ignoring the world for a whole week to focus on a game jam, that's just borrowed time, and the world will catch up with you. Had to sort out a few things.

Anyhow, back to this.

Here are my key takeaways from last week, I hope I'll remember to read them before the next jam:

- This is fun, and I want to try again. I did it the un-recommended way: with long dev hours, total commitment, and burning myself out completely by the end. I don't mind it, actually that's kind of what makes it fun. It's a challenge, and as such I should be pushed to at least some of my limits. Obviously I could not do this every month, but once or twice a year should be fine.  
- I should either try to find a team or at least an artist, or come up with an idea, that can be accomplishes with a font and at most 8 sprites. My initial idea was not that far from achievable. It was perfectly possible in a normal pace for one coder and one artist, or maybe with a little overtime by a coder who knows how to do art and UI passably.
- No content. By this I mean ideas that rely on having a lot of texts, a lot of sound, a lot of sprites/models. Creating a lot of anything takes up a lot of time, and that time should be spent on gameplay. Or graphics. Or whatever makes the game unique. 
- Think small, so-so small. A single gameplay idea. If I did a 7 day game jam again, I would pick something that I think is achievable in 4 days. that gives me 3 days to have fun with my prototype and add things to it.
- No asset packs. I know it should be fine, and they can in theory help with rapid prototyping, but in practice they don't. Asset pack hunting is soul-crushing, does not help with prototyping (anything can be achieved with badly drawn home-made assets, or even 8 squares of different colors). 

In the meantime I'll continue with the prototype, give myself 2-3 weeks to make it not just functional, but hopefully fun as well. I'll also keep the blog going, though not daily, but maybe a post at each milestone. First I decided to take some time to refactor, because I don't want to spend 2 weeks in a codebase I hate. I expect it to take 2 days, and timeboxing it to 4.
